## 1. The Club / Place / Yard

Tactile. Workshop, swap-meet, garage, homebrew club. The name is a location you go to.

### Forkyard
**FORK-yard** · compound · 8 letters

The lot where people take a copy, tinker, and show the build. Teaches the verb as a place, which is what the brief now allows.

| Usage | Read |
|---|---|
| “I found it on Forkyard” | Native. Sounds like a Saturday lot. |
| “Forkyard status” | Incident-page clean. |
| Win95 title | `FORKYARD.EXE — Today's Leaderboard` |

**Scorecard:** Strategic 5 · Audience 5 · Distinct 4 · Tone 5 · Stretch 5 · **~90**
**Risk:** Fort Worth food-truck park **The Fork Yard** (Hemphill St., Star-Telegram Dec 2025) is real common-law use of the same gathering-place metaphor. **FORKYA!** is a live Class 9 diet-app filing. Exact `.com` is unlikely to be cheap. “Fork” still reads as git to developers; one line of product copy (“you get a copy you can change”) covers consumers. Advance, with a hard common-law check on the Fort Worth use.

## What I’d actually put on the building

**1. Forkyard**

It is a place. People can say “see you at the yard.” The verb and the club sit in the same word, which is the homebrew lineage without saying Homebrew. It already survives every usage test in the brief, including `Connect to Forkyard over SSH` and a Win95 title bar. The editorial column (“Nate’s Software”) can live *inside* a yard; a yard cannot live inside a magazine.

The Fort Worth **The Fork Yard** is the thing to kill or live with before you print stickers. If counsel says that common-law use is a real fight in consumer services, drop to 2.

**2. Copyhold**

If “fork” is the problem (food park, FORKYA!, git-nerd trap), this is the ownership thesis with no developer vocabulary. The historical fact is almost rude in how well it fits: the copy is the title. It stretches to marketplace, creator payouts, and the git engine without changing register. Slightly less Saturday-morning than Forkyard; more deed than lot. That is a tone choice, not a hole.

**3. Forklore**

If the emotional center is lineage (features moving through families, money flowing to ancestors), this is the one people will tell other people. It makes the editorial route feel native. Infrastructure still works (`forklore status`, SSH, docs) if the chrome is played straight.

The FORGELORE Class 42 filing is the clearance gate. If that mark sticks in software services, Forklore gets expensive.

**Round 2 if the top three all take a hit:** Swaplot (place, no fork), Afterkin (lineage, coined, likely cleaner), Toft (short, ownable, must pass a say-spell test).

I would not lead with the chrome-only set (Drivebay, Clack, Etch). They sit in the UI. They do not carry a 10-year marketplace plus a merge queue.

**Decision owner still needs to lock** launch geography, TM risk appetite, and domain budget before anyone spends money. The brief already says that; the Fort Worth collision makes it non-optional for Forkyard.
