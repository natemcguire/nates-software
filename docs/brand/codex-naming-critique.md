## 1. Critique of Grok’s candidates

### The process problem

Grok’s exploration is imaginative, but its scoring creates false confidence:

- It effectively scores five dimensions while the brief requires nine, particularly understating trademark, digital, linguistic, and verbal-performance risk.
- It calls names “screened” while acknowledging that formal trademark and domain searches were not conducted.
- The Win95 title-bar mockups test visual compatibility, not whether someone can hear, remember, trust, recommend, or understand the name.
- The pool over-indexes archaic craft, botanical lineage, and property terminology. That makes the brand feel researched rather than intuitive.
- Two of its top three contain “fork,” leaving the recommendation dangerously concentrated in one technical metaphor.

## 3. Definitive current top five

### 1. Ownhand

**Ownhand is the strongest masterbrand because it compresses the two most valuable promises—ownership and authorship—into one short, ordinary-English construction.**

It makes the platform’s complex infrastructure emotionally legible. Git provenance, safe AI modification, version families, creator revenue, and portability all serve one outcome: the customer becomes more than a renter or user. They gain a hand in the product and a credible claim over their version.

It also has several structural strengths:

- Consumer-first rather than mechanism-first.
- Seven letters, two syllables, easy to say and visually compact.
- Human and tactile, balancing the otherwise automated AI proposition.
- Appropriate for the retro workshop/marketplace aesthetic without being trapped inside it.
- Broad enough for the marketplace, creator tools, infrastructure, editorial products, and future categories.
- It supports a strong explanatory lockup: **Ownhand — software, made yours.**

The usage architecture works:

- “I got it on Ownhand.”
- “Publish your app on Ownhand.”
- “See every Ownhand version and the creators behind it.”
- “Ownhand for Creators.”
- “Ownhand Status.”
- “Connect to Ownhand over SSH.”

Commercially, `OwnHand.com` is currently listed for sale at $3,895, meaning the exact `.com` is at least obtainable rather than occupied by an operating product. Do not acquire it until counsel clears the name, and expect the price to change. [Current domain listing](https://ownhand.com/)

Its principal risk is category inference: without a descriptor, some people will think craft marketplace. That is testable. If “handmade goods” dominates user testing, Altermake should become the lead.

### 2. Altermake

The cleanest articulation of the actual product behavior: take a working thing and change it. More distinctive and potentially more protectable than Ownhand, but colder and less explicit about ownership.

### 3. Wholekit

Best expression of completeness and portability. It directly counters SaaS rental and thin AI wrappers. Its weakness is that a “kit” can sound unfinished, so testing must establish whether people hear “everything included” or “assembly required.”

### 4. Made Yours

The strongest pure consumer promise and the best messaging benchmark. I would prefer it as the tagline—**“Software, made yours”**—unless counsel finds an unexpectedly strong registration path.

### 5. Shiftmade

A credible transformation name with useful energy and adequate stretch. It remains less ownable semantically than the top three and requires careful current-use review.

Forkyard would rank approximately third on strategy alone. Once actual current use is incorporated, it falls outside the recommended five.

## 4. Clearance and next steps

### Immediate decision

Advance only these names into paid clearance:

1. **Ownhand**
2. **Altermake**
3. **Wholekit**

Carry **Made Yours** as the messaging control and likely tagline. Retain **Shiftmade** as an emergency backup.

Retire Copyhold, Forklore, Afterkin, Swaplot, Makekeep, and the specialist botanical/craft vocabulary. Put Forkyard on hold only if counsel believes the Fort Worth use can safely coexist; otherwise retire it.

### Before trademark work

Write a one-page product-rights memo defining exactly what “own” means:

- Is source provided?
- Is the buyer receiving title, an exclusive license, or a perpetual nonexclusive license?
- What survives cancellation?
- Can the version be exported and hosted elsewhere?
- Which upstream licenses constrain modification or resale?
- What rights attach to paid inherited features?

Ownhand or “Made Yours” becomes legally and reputationally dangerous if the commercial terms provide only revocable access.

### Legal knockout

Have trademark counsel search:

- Exact, spaced, phonetic, plural, and visually similar forms.
- US federal live and dead marks, state registrations, and common-law use.
- Initially Classes 9 and 42; evaluate Classes 35 for the marketplace and 36 for payments or creator-revenue distribution.
- WIPO/EUIPO and priority launch markets if international expansion is plausible.

Important variants include:

- OWNHAND / OWN HAND / ONEHAND / ONHAND
- ALTERMAKE / ALTER MAKE / ALTERMADE
- WHOLEKIT / WHOLE KIT / THE WHOLE KIT
- SHIFTMADE / SHIFT MADE

### Digital clearance

For every surviving name, investigate:

- Domain ownership, price, prior use, backlinks, and archive history.
- GitHub organizations and repositories.
- npm, PyPI, crates.io, and package registries.
- Apple, Google Play, Microsoft, and browser-extension stores.
- Product Hunt and startup databases.
- Major social handles and support/status subdomains.

Use a broker or escrow service for acquisition and avoid approaching existing users directly before counsel’s opinion.

### Audience testing

Run monadic testing—not a beauty-contest ballot—with roughly 12–16 nontechnical buyers and 6–8 developers/creators.

Show one name at a time with the same plain-language descriptor, then measure:

- Category inference.
- What ownership promise they believe is being made.
- Say/spell accuracy after hearing it once.
- Unaided recall the next day.
- Trust, personality, and willingness to recommend.
- Whether Ownhand means software agency or “Etsy for crafts.”
- Whether Wholekit means complete product or assembly required.

My recommended winner condition is at least 80% correct spelling after hearing, strong next-day recall, and no dominant unintended category association.

Once one name passes counsel and testing, secure the domain and principal handles together, then file intent-to-use applications. Until then, **Ownhand is the strategic leader—not yet a cleared name.**
