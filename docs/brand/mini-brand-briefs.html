Mini Brand Briefs — The Seven Bets

Companion to the full Naming Brand Brief (the marketplace masterbrand doc). All generic rules from that brief apply to every bet below — linguistic requirements (1 word preferred, 2–4 syllables, hear-say-spell test), the evaluation scorecard, and the practical screens (.com/.dev, USPTO Class 9/42, linguistic disaster-check). These minis carry only what is specific to each product: who it's for, what register the name must hit, the real namescape from the research, territories worth exploring, hard screens, and a verdict on the current working name. Aug 25 2026.

Umbrella note: each of these scopes a standalone product brand, optionally endorsed by East Bay Projects ("an East Bay Projects product") — none needs to carry the others. The one masterbrand-scale naming job in the portfolio (the marketplace) already has the full brief.

1. DealScope — pre-LOI dossiers for SMB acquirers

Assignment

Name a high-touch research service selling $950–1,500 pre-LOI dossiers on SMB sellers to search-fund and ETA buyers, with a later SaaS tier for licensed diligence shops. The name is the product a buyer forwards to their investment committee — it must survive an email subject line and an invoice.

Positioning & promise

For acquisition searchers stuck between $49 robo-reports and $5–25K consultants: senior-grade seller intelligence, before you sign anything. Promise: know who you're really buying from.

Buyer & register

MBA-educated searchers, independent sponsors, family offices. Register: forensic, senior, calm — a name that sounds like a firm, not an app.

Aim forDo not drift into
Rigorous, discreet, seniorSpy/surveillance creepiness
Decision-grade clarityRobo-report cheapness ("insta-", "-ly")
Finance-literateBro-finance swagger

Namescape

DueDilio, SearchFunder, Acquire.com, Grata, PrivSource. Crowded behavior: literal deal/diligence/search compounds. Whitespace: the person behind the business angle — nobody names for seller scrutiny.

Territories

Optics & scrutiny (lens, aperture, second look) · field craft & provenance (the file, the record) · pre-commitment ritual (before ink, first light). Directions, not candidates.

Hard screens (product-specific)

FCRA line: the name and tagline must never evoke consumer reporting, background checks, or people-screening — the research cleared this business only in the investment/M&A lane. Kill any candidate a stranger could read as "background check service." Also avoid "diligence" itself: generic, and it drifts toward the screening register.

Working-name verdict: SCREEN, likely replace. "DealScope" is serviceable but sits in the crowded deal-compound lane and sounds like a $49 SaaS, not a $1,500 service. A firm-like evocative name would price better.

2. Frostline — weather-true garden planner

Assignment

Name a consumer subscription app ($24.99/yr) that plans a vegetable garden against live local weather rather than static zone maps, wedging in via a free frost-date widget.

Positioning & promise

For home vegetable gardeners burned by generic zone advice: planting plans that track your actual sky. Promise: plant on your weather, not a map from 1990.

Buyer & register

Serious hobby gardeners 30–65, mostly women, already paying GrowVeg-type tools. Register: almanac-trustworthy with modern precision — Field & Stream, not Silicon Valley.

Aim forDo not drift into
Grounded, seasonal, preciseCutesy garden clichés (sprout/bloom/seed)
Weather-literate authorityWeather-app genericism
Warm, patientTwee cottagecore

Namescape

GrowVeg, Seedtime, Planter, From Seed to Spoon (→ ParkSeed), Gardenate. Crowded behavior: grow/seed/plant compounds. Whitespace: climate/weather vocabulary — almost untouched in the category.

Territories

Threshold moments (frost line, last frost, first light) · field wisdom (almanac register) · microclimate pride (your yard as a place with a name).

Hard screens (product-specific)

"Frostline" has real-world collisions to check: a frozen-treat franchise (Frostline soft-serve mix) and possible outdoor-gear marks — verify Class 9/42 specifically. Must also work in Southern-hemisphere/mild-climate markets where frost isn't the constraint.

Working-name verdict: KEEP, pending screen. "Frostline" is the strongest working name in the portfolio — a real term, the exact wedge, zero category cliché. Screen the collisions before falling further in love.

3. CouncilGraph — local-government intelligence

Assignment

Name a B2B subscription ($199/mo, Austin-first) that mines council agendas, minutes, and filings into a who-influences-what relationship graph for land-use lawyers, lobbyists, and developers. Free weekly digest is the top of funnel — the name lives on that digest.

Positioning & promise

For professionals whose deals die in city hall: know what's moving, and who moves it, before the agenda drops. Promise: the insider's read, without being an insider.

Buyer & register

Land-use attorneys, government-affairs consultants, developers. Register: authoritative, plugged-in, a touch wry — capitol-press-corps energy, not civic-tech-nonprofit energy (that register signals "no budget").

Aim forDo not drift into
Insider, early, sharpCivic-nonprofit earnestness
Record-backed authorityEnterprise GovTech beige
Local textureParochial smallness (must scale past Austin)

Namescape

Hamlet, Cloverleaf.ai, FiscalNote, Ontopical, Curate. Crowded behavior: abstract friendly words (Hamlet, Cloverleaf) or fiscal/policy compounds. Whitespace: the dais itself — quorum, gavel, minutes, the room where it happens.

Territories

Chamber furniture (dais, quorum, gavel, docket) · early signal (first reading, posted notice) · the record (minutes, ledger of influence).

Hard screens (product-specific)

Avoid "-graph"/"-scope"/"-AI" mechanics suffixes (the full brief's rule applies doubly here — buyers are lawyers, allergic to startup-sounding vendors). Nothing that implies lobbying services or PAC activity — it's intelligence, not influence.

Working-name verdict: REPLACE. "CouncilGraph" describes the database, not the advantage, and "graph" dates it. The digest needs a masthead-quality name — something a lawyer forwards without explaining.

4. TrueHue — calibration-card paint matching

Assignment

Name a consumer app ($12.99 one-time + $7 printed calibration card) that turns a phone photo into an accurate paint match via real colorimetry (ΔE math), attacking Pantone-style color rent and $349 Nix hardware.

Positioning & promise

For homeowners and makers who've been burned by eyeballed matches: lab-honest color from your phone. Promise: the match you can measure.

Buyer & register

DIY homeowners, hobby painters, small decorators. Register: confident, measurable, a little rebellious toward the color establishment — "we show our math."

Aim forDo not drift into
Measured, honest, exactPaint-brand softness (swatch-speak)
Physics prideLab-coat sterility
Anti-gatekeeper spiritAnti-Pantone axe-grinding in the name itself

Namescape

Nix, Datacolor, Pantone, ColorSnap (Sherwin-Williams), Color Muse. Crowded behavior: color/hue/chroma compounds everywhere; every paint brand has a "color + verb" app.

Territories

Calibration & proof (the card, delta, true north) · light itself (spectra, noon light) · the honest instrument (level, plumb, tuning fork — tools that don't lie).

Hard screens (product-specific)

Philips Hue: Signify holds "Hue" marks in software-adjacent classes and enforces them; "TrueHue" sits phonetically on top of a famous mark in a color context — treat as a probable conflict, not a maybe (screening item: confirm scope of Signify's registrations before any attachment). "True" + color words are also heavily used across paint apps.

Working-name verdict: REPLACE, pending the physics validation anyway. Do the ΔE test first; if the product lives, name it from the calibration/proof territory — the card is the brand.

5. Rehearsal Receipt — AI rehearsal recap for bands

Assignment

Name a passion-sized product ($49–79/yr per band) that records a rehearsal and hands the band a structured artifact: songs played, takes, riffs worth keeping, who said what to work on.

Positioning & promise

For working and hobby bands who lose Tuesday's best idea by Thursday: proof of what happened in the room. Promise: never lose the good take again.

Buyer & register

Gigging hobby bands, worship teams, rehearsal-space regulars — musicians with jobs. Register: musician-to-musician, wry, zero corporate; merch-table energy.

Aim forDo not drift into
In-joke warmth, band-room slangMusic-edtech blandness
Tangible artifact ("the receipt")Pro-audio jargon (DAW-speak)
Scrappy, loyalStartup gloss

Namescape

BandLab, Moises, LANDR, Tonara, Practice Space. Crowded behavior: band/track/tone compounds and producer-tool names. Whitespace: the social ritual of rehearsal — nobody names for the room and the recap.

Territories

Proof & artifact (receipt, setlist, tape) · band-room ritual (load-in, count-in, one-more-time) · memory (the take, the keeper).

Hard screens (product-specific)

Two-word names are fine here (consumer, spoken aloud at practice), but check "receipts" slang stays friendly (it means evidence against you in internet usage — in-band that's the joke; confirm it reads as fun, not surveillance of bandmates).

Working-name verdict: KEEP. "Rehearsal Receipt" is alliterative, self-explaining, and funny in exactly the register the buyer lives in. Screen it, don't second-guess it.

6. Heritage Dossier — verified family-history gift reports

Assignment

Name a high-touch gift service ($500 / $950 / $1,500 tiers) producing family-history reports where every claim is cited to a primary record — sitting in the unserved gap between $150 Etsy charts and $2,950 LegacyTree.

Positioning & promise

For adult children buying a once-ever gift for a parent: a beautiful, verified family story — not a mug with a crest. Promise: the story is true, and here's the proof.

Buyer & register

Gift-givers 35–60 at milestone moments (anniversaries, 70th birthdays, memorials). Register: warm, archival, permanent — heirloom bookbinding, not genealogy software.

Aim forDo not drift into
Heirloom warmth, permanenceFuneral-home solemnity
Archival credibility (cited, verified)Forensic coldness
Gift-shaped generosityGenealogy-hobbyist jargon (GEDCOM-land)

Namescape

StoryWorth, Ancestry, LegacyTree, Storied, MyHeritage. Crowded behavior: story/legacy/heritage/roots/tree — the entire obvious vocabulary is taken or generic. Whitespace: the object register (what it feels like to hold) and the proof register (verified, cited).

Territories

The heirloom object (folio, volume, keepsake press) · the keeper role (archivist as a gift) · proof-as-love (attested, on record).

Hard screens (product-specific)

Register tension to resolve in naming: "Dossier" is forensic-cold for a love purchase — it names what Nate builds, not what the parent receives. "Heritage" is simultaneously generic in the category (MyHeritage) and unprotectable. The name must sell at the moment of gift-wrapping.

Working-name verdict: REPLACE. Keep "dossier" as internal craft language; brand the gift from the heirloom-object territory.

7. Restitch — upstream template changes, replayed as PRs

Assignment

Name a GitHub App ($15–25/private repo/mo, team $299/mo) that replays upstream template/boilerplate changes as tested, feature-level adopt-or-skip PRs onto long-diverged repos — "Dependabot for your starting point." Org/platform-team wedge first.

Positioning & promise

For platform teams whose golden-path templates drift dead within a quarter: upstream improvements keep arriving, as reviewable PRs. Promise: your starting point stays alive.

Buyer & register

Platform/DX engineers and eng managers; discovered via GitHub Marketplace and word-of-mouth. Register: craftsmanlike, calm, trustworthy-with-your-repo — Dependabot's civility, not agent-hype.

Aim forDo not drift into
Craft & repair (mending metaphor)Cutesy sewing-kit overload
Test-gated dependabilityAutonomous-agent hype ("AI dev")
Lineage awarenessfork/merge/sync literalism (full brief's rule)

Namescape

Dependabot, Renovate, CodeRabbit, Mergify, cruft, Copier, Moderne. Crowded behavior: bot-suffixes, verb-tools (Renovate), mascot names (CodeRabbit). Whitespace: repair/craft vocabulary — Renovate is the only neighbor, and it owns dependencies, not templates.

Territories

Mending (stitch, graft, darn, reweave) · living lineage (descendants kept current) · the workshop (bench, jig — tools that make other tools true).

Hard screens (product-specific)

Check npm, PyPI, and GitHub org collisions hard — dev-tool names die on registry squatting. "Restitch" specifically: screen existing repos/packages and any embroidery-software marks (ironically, StitchVector's research territory). Must read cleanly in restitch.yml, a PR title, and "Restitch opened 3 PRs."

Working-name verdict: KEEP, pending registry screen. "Restitch" is doing real work: repair metaphor, implies pre-existing fabric (your diverged repo), git-adjacent without saying git. Best-fit name after Frostline.

Process note: these minis feed the full brief's pipeline — territory map → 250 raw candidates → screened rounds — per bet, when each bet earns naming investment (post-validation, pre-launch). Verdict summary: Keep Frostline, Rehearsal Receipt, Restitch (screen all three) · Replace CouncilGraph, TrueHue, Heritage Dossier · Screen-likely-replace DealScope.
